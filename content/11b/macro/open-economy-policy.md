# economic policy in open economy

* trade deficit from $G$ raising $Y$ causing more foreign goods to be bought
![[open-economy-1.png|400]]

* foreign output $Y^*$, effects on domestic economy
    * foreign government spending $G^*$ could be taking place, etc.
    * first assume not knowing where $Y^*$ came from
* domestic demand for goods, $C + I + G$
    * function of income
    * drawn as $DD$, steeper than $ZZ$
    * difference between $ZZ$ and $DD$ is net exports, $NX$
        * if trade is balanced at point A, both intersect there
* effects of foreign output increase $\Delta Y^*$
    * higher foreign output -> higher foreign demand
        * higher foreign demand -> higher foreign demand for domestic goods
        * increase in foreign output causes increase in domestic exports, by amount $\Delta X$
    * net exports $NX$ and $ZZ$ both go up by $\Delta X$
![[open-economy-2.png|400]]

* $NX$ is always distance between $ZZ$ and $DD$ at given $Y$
* increase in $Y^*$ always shifts $ZZ$ up, $DD$ below $ZZ$ means surplus

* domestic demand increase -> increase output, bad $NX$
* foreign demand increase -> increase output, better $NX$

* real exchange rate given as $\epsilon = \frac{EP}{P^*}$
    * equal to nominal exchange rate $E$ times price level $P$ divided by foreign price level $P^*$

* real depreciation -> fall in $\epsilon$
    * means domestic goods are cheaper compared to foreign goods

* $NX = X - \frac{IM}{\epsilon}$
* $NX = X(Y^*, \epsilon) - IM(Y, \epsilon)/\epsilon$
    * very dependent on real depreciation in exports, imports, relative price ($\frac{1}{\epsilon}$)
    * decrease in $\epsilon$ causes increase in $NX$ by Marshall-Lerner condition (assumed true)

* depreciation leads to shift in demand, both foreign and domestic, towards domestic goods
    * leads to increase in domestic output, improvement in trade balance

* fixing $NX$ without changing output requires
    * achieving depreciation to eliminate it, to shift $NX$ to $NX'$
    * reduce government spending to shift $ZZ'$ back to $ZZ$
![[open-economy-3.png|400]]

* real depreciation leads to deterioration then improvement in J-curve
![[open-economy-4.png|400]]

* $Y = C + I + G - IM/\epsilon + X$
* $S = I + G - T - IM/\epsilon + X$
* $NX = S + (T - G) - I$

* increase in government spending leads to increase in output, increase in interest rate, real appreciation
![[open-economy-5.png|400]]

* monetary contraction causes causes appreciation as A shifts up to A'
![[open-economy-6.png|400]]

* under fixed exchange rate, domestic interest rate equals foreign interest rate, causes necessary monetary accomodation to hold $i$
![[open-economy-7.png|400]]

* monetary expansion causes $LM$ down, $Y$ up, $i$ down, depreciation, $NX$ improves
    * helps both $Y$ and $NX$
* fiscal policy more powerful under fixed $E$, forced monetary accomodation
