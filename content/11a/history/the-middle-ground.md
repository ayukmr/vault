# The Middle Ground - Commerce & Violence

* Violence and murder correlated with commerce
* In 1684 alone, 39 Frenchmen died at hands of Algonquian allies
    * Murdered during robberies, in disputes, avenging, etc
* French traders labeled indigenous societies as thieves
* Collective responsibility only came if murderer not disclosed
    * Group of family, kin, village, nation

* Dulhut helped orchestrate alliance between Saulteurs and Sioux
* Inflamed people of Green Bay against French
    * Already attempted to block French trade with Sioux
    * Fox fought and defeated Sioux-Chippewa force
* In turmoil, party with at least one Menominee murdered 2 Frenchmen
    * Menominee are a member of a Green Bay tribe
* Local Algonquian people offered cultural procedures
    * Dulhut rejected validity of exchanges
    * Dulhut insisted on penalty of blood revenge instead
* Cultural hybrids in French process of establishing peace
* Dulhut unilaterally decided to execute party of murderers
    * French wintering at Keweenaw were appalled, feared revenge
* Blood revenge was appropriate in both societies
    * For Algonquians, death at hands of enemies v. hands of allies
    * For French, killings in wars v. murders
        * Killing enemies in war is justifiable as separate
* Dulhut had the two Indians executed, was seen as two more murders
* Entire situation was indecisive in terms of the killings
