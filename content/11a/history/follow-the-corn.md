# Follow the Corn

* 12,000 years ago - people stay put and developed ag
* 8500 BC - Domestication of plants in seven locales
    * Three in the Americas
        * Valley of Mexico, Central America
        * South-Central Andes in South America
        * Eastern North America
* Indigenous ag was based on corn
    * Capacity of grain production impressed Europeans
* First great cultivators of corn were the Mayans
    * Religion was constructed around the food
    * Mayans developed art, architecture, sculpture, etc.
* 1426, Aztecs formed alliance with Texcoco and Tlacopan
    * Aztecs emerged as dominant in Triple Alliance
        * Economic basis for state was "hydraulic ag"
        * Dominance waned during Spanish intrusion
* 1250 BC, communities in southern Arizona began irrigation
* Muskogees, Seminoles, other poeple in Southeast
    * Three branches of government
        * Civil administration
        * Military
        * "Sacred-dealing"
* 1492 North America was network of indigenous nations

## Thesis

Major advances in agricultural technology by indigenous populations—especially shown in the focus on production of corn—allowed for sustaining larger populations and led to further advances in civilizations.

I wondered if the prominence of corn could have been replicated with a different crop if it had similar properties, since corn was significant in other contexts like religion compared to other foods.
