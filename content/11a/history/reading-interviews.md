## Encounters and Trade in the Early Atlantic World

* Fur trade between indigenous and Europeans (French, British, Spanish, Dutch)
* Challenges myth that indigenous were "passive" during expansion
    * Argues Europeans were not "destined" to take over the continent
* Indigenous had economic power over the Europeans
    * Had access to beaver fur, gave negotiating power
* Europeans would give cotton, other clothing items in exchange for beaver fur and fish
    * Indigenous demand for cotton shaped first plantations
        * Plantations produced cotton since demanded cotton for trading
* Was a mutually beneficial trade relationship, not typically presented that way
    * Europeans used beaver fur used to create hats, symbol of status
    * Indigenous used cotton for creating native dresses
* Europeans had significant degree of respect for the indigenous culture

> "During French tenure in the Great Lakes, fur traders and Indians met on a common ground of exchange ..."

## Borders and Borderlands

* Indigenous did not have clearly defined borders
    * Civilization was heavily decentralized
    * Borders were not static, had unclear definition based on landmark etc
        * Still very much in fluxw

* When Europeans arrived, bumped into borders of indigenous
* Founder of Pennsylvania bought land from indigenous
    * People bought from did not have formal states to the lands
* Europeans began to take land as unclaimed
    * Presented themselves as explorers

## The Power of Weakness

* Europeans and indigenous can coexist
    * Europeans do not solely hold power
* Benefits to both French and indigenous through trade
* Jesuit missionary believed French were weak and needed indigenous to survive

## Gender Roles and Marriage

* Pushes back on indigenous as primitive society
* Brings forth idea that French and indigenous could coexist
* French women did not come to colonies
    * Indigenous women had social power over French men
    * Marriage would create alliances
* French instilled Christian values onto marriage
    * Shifted dynamic to become more patriarchal

## Encounter and Trade in the Early Atlantic World

* Early European presence roughly in 1500s
    * First came primarily for cod fishing
    * French, English, Spanish, Portuguese
* Catholicism imposed fish-based diet
    * Cured fish had a very long shelf life
* First just brought fish back, then traded with indigenous
* Indigenous women provided medicine to prevent scurvy
* After cod fishing, beaver pelts
    * Symbol of wealth and status
    * Traded beaver pelt for cloth
