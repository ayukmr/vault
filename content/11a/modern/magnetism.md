## bats

$B = \mu_0I \div 2 \pi r$ (given)
$\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7}$ (given)
$r_h = 0.02$ m (head size)

---

Q: The magnetic field strength near Earth’s surface is $B_E = 50 \times 10^{-6}$ T. If you'd like to disrupt the magnetoreception of a bat using a straight wire placed outside the bat brain, approximately how much current should you pass through the wire?

$B_E = 50 \times 10^{-6} = \mu_0I \div 2 \pi r$
$50 \times 10^{-6} = (4 \pi \times 10^{-7})I \div 0.04 \pi$
$(50 \times 10^{-6} \times 0.04 \pi) \div (4 \pi \times 10^{-7}) = I$
$I = 5$ A

---

Q: This is a rather large amount of current and probably not safe for the bats. The field strength at the center of a circular loop of current-carrying wire is $B = \mu_0 I \div 2R$, where $R$ is the radius of the loop. If you limit yourself to $I = 30 mA$, how can you arrange the wire to create a field of magnitude $B_E$?

$B_E = 50 \times 10^{-6} = \mu_0I \div 2R$
$50 \times 10^{-6} = (4 \pi \times 10^{-7})(0.03) \div 2R$
$(4 \pi \times 10^{-7})(0.03) \div (50 \times 10^{-6}) = 2R$
$R = 0.0014$
