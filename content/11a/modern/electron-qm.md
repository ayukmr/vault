1. The electrons are initially accelerated across a potential difference of $\Delta V = 200$ volts. Look up the charge and mass of the electron, and determine the final velocity (**in kilometers/second**) using conservation of energy. You may assume they are at rest before moving across this potential difference.

$\Delta U = q \Delta V$

$q = 1.602 \times 10^{-19}$ C (given)
$\Delta V = 200$ V (given)

$\Delta U = 1.602 \times 10^{-19} \times 200$
$\Delta U = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$

$m = 9.109 \times 10^{-31}$ kg (given)

$1.602 \times 10^{-19} \times 200 = \frac{1}{2} \times 9.109 \times 10^{-31} \times v^2$
$v = 8387370$ m/s

---

2. Within the Helmholtz coil, the magnetic field is approximately $B = 960$ uT. Use your knowledge of circular motion and the magnetic force to predict the radius (**in centimeters**) of the electron's path.

$F = qvBsin\theta$

$B = 960$ uT (given)
$F = (1.602 \times 10^{-19}) \times 8387370 \times (960 \times 10^{-6})$

$F = mv^2 \div r$
$(1.602 \times 10^{-19}) \times 8387370 \times (960 \times 10^{-6}) = (9.109 \times 10^{-31}) \times 8387370^2 \div r$

$r = 0.0496778$ m

---

3. Suppose we repeated this experiment with protons. The magnitude of the charge is the same, but the mass is different from an electron. Predict the radius (**in meters**) of a proton's circular path.

$v = \sqrt{2 \times q \Delta V \div m}$
$qB = mv \div r$

$m = 1.6726 \times 10^{-27}$ kg (given)

v = $\sqrt{2 \times (1.602 \times 10^{-19}) \times 200 \div (1.6726 \times 10^{-27})}$
$v = 195734$

$(1.602 \times 10^{-19}) \times (960 \times 10^{-6}) = (1.6726 \times 10^{-27}) \times 195734 \div r$
$r = 2.12875$ m
