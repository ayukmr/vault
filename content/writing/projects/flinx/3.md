# PCB

_3 hours_

I routed the PCB for my keyboard.

The first step was just laying out all the keys. As with a normal keyboard, most of the keys are 1us with a few larger keys on the side. However, an odd feature of this keyboard is the spacebar, which is made up of 4 1.75u keys. I did this to avoid having to use stabilizers, and to add a bit of uniqueness to the keyboard's design, since other than that, the layout is basically the same as the Monokei Neko.

In the bottom middle, I put the nRF chip and the caps, resistors, and inductor it needs. The wiring is pretty complex, but I was able to use vias to provide an easy way to get the column/row pins out, along with a few other ones.

![[nrf.png|400]]

To the right, I put the antenna, with its necessary inductors and keepout zone. It took a bit to get this right, since I made extra sure that the layout corresponded with the manufacturer's diagram.

![[antenna.png|400]]

To the left of the nRF chip, I put the battery pads and its TP4057 charger.

![[bat.png|400]]

On the right side of the PCB, I put the USB-C port and the switch for having the battery connected.

![[right.png|200]]

Most of the complexity just came from the many different overlapping traces, but I'm pretty happy with how the PCB turned out.
